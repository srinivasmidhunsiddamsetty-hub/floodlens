# Design decisions

A log of the product, visual and engineering choices in FloodLens and why each was made. The case-study framing is in [../CASE_STUDY_NOTES.md](../CASE_STUDY_NOTES.md).

## Layout

- **Desktop: a docked left panel, and Insights cards floating over the map (v0.34).** The left panel is 300 px (v0.42; 280 before, widened slightly at the owner's request). The two Insights cards sit over the right of the map with no panel behind them, so the map runs under them and feels less boxed in (review feedback #10). The camera is padded by the cards' width, so the city view, the area chips and "the middle of the map" use the part the cards don't cover; the scale bar and credit stay left of them. History: the cards floated in v0.27–v0.31, were docked on a grey panel to match the left in v0.32–v0.33, and float again from v0.34. Whether the left panel should float too is still open. The Insights width defaults to 380 px (v0.27; 344 before), measured so long titles ("Building near Pennsylvania Avenue", about 364 px) and "Not up to +5 ft" stay on one line. From v0.6 to v0.36 it was resizable (300–560 px, remembered per browser) and collapsed with an edge tab; v0.37 fixed it at 380 px with neither, at the owner's request, and v0.38 narrowed it to 360 px. A very long street name now wraps the card title to two lines. The left panel still collapses with its edge tab.
- **Insights is only about what you pick on the map (v0.36).** The right side holds the selected building or spot's card, or a short "Click any building" prompt. The prompt shows each time FloodLens is opened, until the first pick (v0.56): it's the only sign that buildings can be clicked, but after one pick it has done its job, and closing the card leaves the right side empty for the rest of the visit. v0.53–v0.55 remembered the first pick in the browser and hid the prompt for good, so the owner, having clicked once, never saw it again. The city-wide "Land underwater" card moved to the Sea level card as two figures (owner's spec): one side for the sea level and what it covers, one for the place you picked.
- **Desktop and laptop only (v0.33). FloodLens is made for screens 1024 px wide and up: a map with a panel on each side needs the room. A narrower window scrolls sideways rather than squeezing the map.
- **The legend is a slim vertical colour scale** (bottom-left, v0.34), like the keys in Google Maps: only the sea-level colours, today at the bottom and the top level at the top. No level is highlighted (v0.54; the chosen one was bold before), since the slider already shows it. Its title is "Water reaches at" (v0.54; "Water first reaches" before), the place card's words; without a title, "+3 ft" beside a colour could read as a depth. It stays at the bottom left (v0.54, the owner's pick): it explains the colours picked in the left panel, and on the right it sat on the scale bar. A minus folds it into a 32 px frosted square showing the colours, which opens it again (v0.54, remembered per browser). Every level keeps its true colour (v0.18). It shows only with Layered colours (v0.35): with one colour there's nothing to decode. Low spots have no row (v0.35; their switch in "On the map" shows the swatch). It takes the pointer (v0.35), so the map's hand cursor and hover labels don't show through it. The other keys (water, building, selected, city limits) went: the switches in "On the map" already show those swatches, and the box sat in the way (feedback #5). It's a plain key (v0.28; the minus is its only control): from v0.3 to v0.27 each level was a hover button that spotlighted its band, but the rows looked selectable, did nothing on click, and levels above the slider didn't respond. Spotlighting moved to the chart and its "adds" row, and went with them in v0.36.

## Map composition (`src/lib/mapStyle.ts`, `src/lib/mapLayers.ts`)

Layer stack from bottom to top:

```
background → land cover / land use → [NOAA extent · comparison · low-lying] → water → roads
→ outside-study mask · study boundary → buildings (extruded or footprints) → labels → selection
```

- NOAA overlays sit **beneath basemap water**. Ocean and bay therefore stay "water", and blue appears where NOAA's extent reaches land. The data are unchanged.
- The **outside-study mask** dims everything beyond the city limits, so "no data" is never read as "no flooding".
- **Buildings:** extrusions in 3D, translucent footprints and outlines in 2D, so overlays under buildings stay visible from above.
- **Labels** stay above every overlay and use a white halo for legibility on the blue bands.
- **The sea-level slider stands upright (v0.34):** today at the bottom and +5 ft at the top, so raising it reads as the sea rising (feedback #2). It's still a native range input (vertical writing mode, `aria-orientation="vertical"`), with the level names beside its stops and the big "+2 ft" to its right. v0.34's play button ("Step up to +5 ft") and the (i) note went in v0.35 at the owner's request: the caption "above today's high tide" says what the number means, and "About the data" explains the levels. The slider gives up height on short windows (168 px down to 116 px), so the left panel fits without scrolling in windows down to about 650 px tall, the browser window of a 1366 × 768 laptop (v0.35).
- **Two colour modes (v0.34), Overall and Per foot (v0.47):** a pill toggle beside the "Sea level" title (v0.35; pill-shaped since v0.47, like the level chips). Earlier names: Simple / Detailed (v0.34), Single / Layered (v0.35–v0.36), Overview / Layered (v0.37), Total / Individual (v0.38–v0.40), Total / By level (v0.41–v0.46). The owner wanted names that explain themselves and picked "Overall / Per foot" over "Total / Added", "All at once / Foot by foot" and others ("per" takes the singular, so "per foot", not "per feet"). Overall (the default) draws all the water at the chosen level in one blue; Per foot gives the area each foot adds its own colour (feedback #9). The map, the slider track, the Flooding swatch and the legend follow the mode, and the browser remembers it.
- **The land underwater sits beside the slider (v0.36)**, where the big "+2 ft" was (the bold level name on the slider already says the level). It follows the Total / Individual toggle, so the figure always matches what the map's colours mark:
  - Overall: "Total area covered", everything covered up to the level beyond today's high tide.
  - Per foot: "New area covered", only what the level adds to the one below.
  - The owner's wording (v0.38). Before: "Underwater at +2 ft" / "Newly underwater at +2 ft" (v0.37), "Total submerged area" / "Additional submerged area" (v0.36). "Flooded" would sound like a forecast, so it isn't used.
  - Two figures of equal weight (v0.38, 24 px bold each): the area, and "Share of city land". Each sits in its own box, centred (v0.39). The share's number sits 4 px below the middle of its box (v0.53, the owner's pick over 8 and 12 px; exactly in the middle in v0.52), with the label resting on it (three grid rows, so on the shortest windows the label keeps its room and the number moves down). Since v0.49 they use the premium navy surface (`.surface-navy`), shared with the place card's answer and facts boxes so the two panels read as one design: solid deep navy `#1e3a5f` with white text, given depth the owner's way (a faint radial glow from the top left, a 4.5% grain, a semi-transparent 1 px hairline with a top highlight, and layered soft shadows). The owner wanted the boxes less subtle; solid options were rendered on the real app, and "all navy" won over navy figures with light facts, brand blue, ink and a light tint. In v0.42–v0.48 they used "Soft light" (`#ffffff` → `#f2f4f7`, hairline `#e4e7ec`). The owner found v0.41's pale blue gradient a recognisable "vibe-coded" cliché and picked Soft light over warm paper, cool slate and sea glass; v0.39–v0.40 had a flat grey-blue fill. The boxes are 8 px apart, the same height whenever there's room (each takes an equal share of the column), with 8 px from label to number and 10 px from number to switch. Their hairline is 1.5 px (v0.43), a touch heavier than the card's boxes, and the numbers are 30 px bold (v0.45, the owner's call; 28 px in v0.43–v0.44, 24 px before). The slider grew from 168 to 224 px (v0.41), 250 px (v0.43) and 254 px (v0.45) to keep the boxes equal. The owner found the bare figures of v0.38 read as "text thrown on a white box".
  - At today's high tide the figures read "0 km²", "0 acres" and "0%" (v0.38). Nothing is ever left blank: v0.36–v0.37 showed "None yet".
  - The added figure is the difference between neighbouring totals (`submergedM2` in `lib/manifest.ts`), not the area of the difference polygons the map draws, so the added figures sum exactly to the total. The two differ by under 1.3% (slivers dropped in processing).
  - Both shares divide by the city's Census land area.
  - One unit at a time, with a km² / acres switch under the area number inside its box, in the same style as Total / Individual (v0.39, the owner's request). The column beside the slider is about 142 px wide, too narrow for a large number and the switch side by side, so the switch sits right under it. The choice is remembered (`floodlens.areaUnit`). History: a switch at the bottom of the column (v0.36), a menu after the number (v0.37), both units at once with no control (v0.38).
  - The figure sits beside the slider rather than beside every level (v0.36), which would have needed an extra row and broken the no-scroll fit on short laptops.
  - The low-spot area sits under the Low spots switch while that layer is on ("0.94 km² at +2 ft", in the chosen unit); it's cut off from the sea, so it isn't in the figures. On short windows the slider gives up 16 px for that line.
  - The level chips on the slider are pills with centred names (v0.41; 6 px corners in v0.40, square in v0.39). The Overall / Per foot and km² / acres toggles are pills too (v0.47), so every "pick one" control in the panel looks alike.
  - Today's level is called "0 ft" everywhere (v0.40, the owner's pick from "Now", "0 ft", "Current"): on the slider, the legend, the place card's track and its answer. Every level reads as a number. "Today" was used before; "High tide" is the most exact but too wide for a chip.
  - Short windows, in two steps (v0.44), so the panel fits without scrolling down to about 650 px: at 800 px tall or less (`short`) the cards' padding and the layer rows tighten; only at 725 px or less (`tiny`) do the figure boxes compact, to 24 px numbers, 11 px labels and tighter gaps. The figures stay full size (30 px since v0.45) on every window 726 px tall or more, and the two boxes stay the same height from 750 px up (765 px with the low-spot line). v0.43 compacted the figures to 20 px at 770 px, which shrank them on ordinary laptop windows; the owner caught it.
- **Map controls: one toolbar at the screen's right edge, under the Insights card (v0.47)**, 16 px from the right and bottom edges, so the map itself stays clear. One frosted bar with thin rules between its groups: 2D over 3D | "Entire Miami Beach" | in 3D, turn 30° left or right and tilt 10° up or down | the compass | zoom. Every button is the same 32 px square. On windows 800 px tall or less, where the full bar would reach a place's card, the four turn and tilt arrows fold into one "Turn and tilt" button that opens them beside it (Escape closes them without closing the card); on windows 725 px or less the buttons are 28 px. So it's one column at every height. v0.38–v0.46 had six separate groups that split into two columns on short windows, which the owner found scattered. History: v0.34 had 2D/3D and the house top right and a labelled "Rotate"/"Tilt" panel; v0.35–v0.36 had one column 88 px up with a round dial, which the owner found awkward; v0.37 had the column at the edge of the visible map, left of the card's column. Reviewers found that people didn't discover tilting and rotating, and some trackpads and mice can't do it (feedback #4, #8), so the moves stay as buttons. Every map button shows its name in a tooltip on hover and keyboard focus, opening just left of the column. The full-extent button shows a folded map, "the whole map" (v0.42, the owner's pick). Before: frame corners (v0.40–v0.41, and v0.4–v0.33) and a house (v0.34–v0.39, from review feedback #6), which the owner found misleading, since the button fits the whole city rather than going "home". The tooltip "Entire Miami Beach" says what it does.
- **The compass stays (v0.40, the owner asked whether it's needed).** On a map you can turn, a compass is the standard way to show which way north is and to turn back to north-up (Google Maps, Apple Maps and MapLibre's own controls all have one). FloodLens needs it more than most: the 3D city view starts turned about 15° to fit the island, so without the needle nothing says north isn't up. Google and Apple hide it while the map faces north; here it would show at the start anyway, so it's always shown.
- **Area chips** (top-left) fly to South Beach, Mid-Beach and North Beach and keep 2D/3D. They're frosted glass (88% white, blurred map behind, a soft shadow; v0.25; flat white with a 1 px border before). The chip you pick fills with the accent straight away (v0.27; a 2 px outline before; frosted navy with a white hairline and a neutral shadow since v0.51), not when the camera arrives. Clicking it again, or "Show all", goes back to the whole city with no chip lit; panning by hand hands the highlight back to "nearest area when zoomed in" (by distance to its bookmark, since the areas have no official boundary). A 16 px map gutter is shared with the map controls and legend.
- **Bottom edge:** attribution runs along the very bottom of the visible map with the scale bar above it, at its right (the controls moved into the Insights column in v0.38), and the legend sits on the left. The credit is one short line in a fixed order, "© OpenStreetMap · © OpenMapTiles · OpenFreeMap | Flood data: NOAA | Boundary: US Census", and it folds into an (i) button on the first drag (v0.29), as the OSMF attribution guidelines allow; the (i) and the Data sources dialog keep the full credits findable.
- **The opening view fits the city** to the viewport in Web Mercator (tilted 40°, looking north-north-west). Camera controls act on the map, so they belong on it (ArcGIS, komoot, Google Maps). The header keeps only app-level actions (about, sources).
- **Selection highlight** is drawn 0.4 m wider and 0.6 m taller than the clicked building. Coincident faces z-fight (flicker) while zooming.
- **3D picking** follows the view ray through candidate building volumes (`src/lib/buildingPick.ts`), because a roof click unprojects to the ground behind the building.

## Copy

The voice, rules and a before/after table are in [UX_WRITING.md](UX_WRITING.md): plain, calm, honest, brief and human. FloodLens never says a place is "safe" or "will flood".

## Chart

*The chart, its tile and the "adds" row were removed in v0.36 (their figures moved beside the slider). The notes below are kept as history.*

- **A plain one-line title: "Land underwater by sea level"** (v0.13), in semibold with no icon (v0.26), a step below the bold "Land underwater" header. v0.8–v0.12 used a computed finding as the title ("The biggest jump comes at +3 ft") plus a subtitle; in review that was more text than the tile needed. The unit is set by the toggle in the tile above, and screen readers hear it as part of the chart's name.
- **Selection rings the bar, not the column.** The selected level gets a 2 px accent ring with a 2 px gap, matching the selected area chip, plus an accent axis label. A column-wide box reads like a card or a focus box and hides which mark is chosen; a ring on the mark ties the state to the data. Near-zero bars (today, +1 ft) still show a small capsule on the baseline, and the hit target stays the full column so they're easy to click.
- **No fading for levels above the slider**, in the chart, the breakdown or (since v0.18) the map legend, which used to fade them and say "not shown". The numbers hold whatever the slider shows, and faded bars and rows read as disabled. Hovering a band still fades the others, to spotlight it.
- **One unit at a time.** A km² / acres switch at the top of the section sets the unit for the headline, chart (axis, labels, tooltips, subtitle, screen-reader table) and breakdown. It's a segmented control in the Calcite "solid" style: the chosen unit is filled with the accent. It's a radio group, so it's one Tab stop and the arrow keys switch units. The choice is remembered in the browser, and km² is the default.
- **Low spots follow the map.** While the Low spots layer is on, a "Low spots at +N ft" row sits under the breakdown, behind a divider, with the map's purple hatch, and says they're cut off from the sea and not counted above. With the layer off (the default), the row is hidden. v0.9 showed it always; in review it was clutter for something most people leave off.
- **Rounded bar tops (v0.20).** Each bar's top corners are rounded by 4 px (less for near-zero bars, so they don't vanish). The segments are clipped by a rounded rectangle that runs below the baseline, so the bottom stays square.
- **Small level labels (v0.24).** The x-axis labels (Today, +1 ft … +5 ft) are 10 px, the same as the card's level track, with the selected level in bold. The y-axis numbers and the values above the bars stay at 11 px.
- **One tile for the area figures (v0.14).** The summary and the chart share the km² / acres switch, so they share a tile: the figures at the chosen level, a hairline, then the chart and one row with what the selected foot adds (v0.19; it listed every foot before). The chart already shows every level, the map legend explains the colours, and hovering a bar segment gives any foot's increment, so a single row is enough. v0.12–v0.13 used two tiles. The tile has a border and no shadow, so the selected-place card above stays the one floating element.
- **The panel fits without scrolling.** The building card and the area tile fit at 1440×900 with a place selected, in every state (checked by an end-to-end test).
- **No bottom link (v0.26).** "What this map can and can't tell you" was a pinned footer from v0.13 to v0.25; it was removed to give the content the space. "About the data" in the top bar opens the same dialog. There's no visible "Insights" title (v0.11): the card and block headers say what the panel is, and screen readers still get the heading. The summary and the chart are two blocks: full-bleed sections divided by hairlines, each with a small accent icon and a header, like Calcite's Block. They aren't cards, so they don't compete with the selected-place card above them.
  - **Block 1, "Land underwater":** two figures side by side, the area at the chosen level and the share of the city's land, plus the unit switch.
  - **Block 2, the chart:** the chart and the per-foot breakdown.
  - The "What this map can and can't tell you" link was at the bottom from v0.11 to v0.25 (v0.10 had removed it); v0.26 removed it again.
- **Mouse clicks don't focus bars.** The browser's focus outline is for keyboard users; a click selects without leaving it behind.

## Colour

The selected-place card leads with the answer, emphasised, in a light-blue block. The layout takes cues from a reference the designer supplied, but the wording stays FloodLens's own:
- **Answer:** "WATER REACHES AT" on the left and **+3 ft** (24 px, black) on the right of the same row (v0.15), like the facts below it. The other answers are "0 ft" (v0.40; "Today" before) and, when no mapped level reaches the place, "Up to +5 ft · Doesn't reach it" (v0.30; "Not up to +5 ft" before): the label carries the limit so the plain answer doesn't overclaim, and it fits on one line. No number in the panel is blue: blue is kept for links and selection outlines.
- **Box size:** the box is 316 × 90 px in the default 380 px pane (v0.27; 277 × 90 px at 344 px in v0.23–v0.26; 92 px tall in v0.16–v0.22); it was 277 × 140 px in v0.14 and 277 × 104 px in v0.15. The rows from top to bottom: 8 px padding, the 32 px answer row, a 4 px gap, the 18 px water band, a 6 px gap, 16 px pin labels and 6 px padding. The 6 px above the pin labels can't shrink further: the larger blue pin would run into its label.
- **Level track:** below the answer (see below).
- **Facts:** one column, grouped in one box (v0.42; straight on the card before), premium navy with white text since v0.49. Values are 20 px semibold (v0.41; medium before). A building lists Height, Footprint and Low spot; a spot or open water lists only Low spot (v0.34, feedback #13). From v0.13 to v0.33 Height and Footprint always showed, with "—" for a spot; reviewers found the empty rows confusing. Footprint has a plot-of-land icon (v0.34; a scan frame before). Low spot has a hollow in the ground holding water (v0.38, drawn in Lucide's style since Lucide has none); before it, water drops (v0.37), an arrow down to a line that looked like a download icon (v0.34–v0.36), and a mountain that suggested high ground.
- **Icons sit in tinted tiles (v0.37)**, for a richer, calmer card: a 36 px tile for the place in the title (deep green on pale green since v0.46, matching the selection on the map; vermilion in v0.42–v0.45, charcoal in v0.38–v0.41), a 20 px tile for the coordinates under the title (which replaced the coordinate chip), and 28 px tiles for each fact (blue, violet and green on their own pale tints). The owner picked these sizes over 32 px throughout.
- **Position:** a chip with the position. The distance to the street was dropped in v0.12: the title already names the street, and the distance says nothing about water.

The **level track** works like this:
- **Labels:** each sea level is labelled along a baseline in 10 px type, with no circles (v0.22–v0.23). The first level that reaches the place has a bold label.
- **Water:** a light-blue band rises in a gentle S from the left and stops at the first level that reaches the place. A dashed line drops from its surface to the baseline at that level. It had a small dot on top until v0.51, which the owner removed because it looked like a handle you could drag. When a place is picked, the water flows in from the left once (1.8 s with an even ease, starting 0.2 s after the card slides in; v0.52, 0.7 s in v0.51 was too quick to notice) and the dashed line appears as the water reaches it (v0.51; the owner picked this over a continuous ripple, which would keep pulling the eye). Both use one clip animation, so they stay in step even on a busy main thread. People who turn off motion see the track still. When no level reaches the place, the water runs to the end with no marker.
- **Schematic:** the shape is not a depth, so it's hidden from screen readers; the pins are a list that says, for each level, whether water reaches the place.

v0.4–v0.10 drew a house on rising streams of water instead. v0.11 put the answer in a row with a simpler pin track. v0.12 made it the large lead again. v0.13 kept that layout but went back to v0.11's wording and dropped the details lifted straight from the reference (status labels under every pin, a supporting sentence and a copy button).

## Motion

- The scenario swap fades the overlay in over 450 ms from 12% opacity. The comparison overlay has no animation.
- 2D/3D uses `easeTo` (900 ms); reset uses `flyTo` (1.4 s).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` turns these into instant `jumpTo` / `setData` updates, disables chart transitions and collapses CSS animations.

## Typography

**Satoshi** for the interface (since v0.7): a geometric sans by Indian Type Foundry with a friendlier, more contemporary feel than Roboto, with tabular figures and every symbol FloodLens uses (≈, ², °, →). It's free under the ITF Free Font License, which allows use on your own site but not redistributing the files (for example in a public repository) or converting them. So the app loads it from Fontshare, ITF's official web-font service, and the font files never enter this repo. If Fontshare can't be reached, the UI falls back to the system sans.

Map labels stay in **Roboto** (Regular, Medium and Italic SDF glyphs self-hosted in `public/fonts/`, because OpenFreeMap only serves Noto Sans). MapLibre needs fonts converted to SDF glyph files, and Satoshi's licence doesn't allow format conversion. Roboto's open shapes also hold up better at 10–12 px over a busy map.

Type scale (classes in `src/styles/index.css`): `t-display` 36/700 (sea level) · `t-metric` 28/500 (headline number) · `t-title` 16/500 (the wordmark and panel titles are bold) · `t-heading` 14/700 · `t-label` 13/500 · `t-body` 13/400 · `t-caption` 12/400 · `t-overline` 10.5/700 uppercase, 0.09em tracking. Tabular numerals for all quantities.

**Bolder hierarchy (v0.25).** Titles and the key numbers (the sea level, the card's answer and the two figures) are bold with tighter tracking (−0.03em on large numbers), so the eye lands on them first. Body text stays regular, so bold keeps its meaning. Satoshi loads as a variable font (300–900), so semibold (600) is a real, lighter weight: the card's title and "Land underwater" moved from 600 to bold in v0.26, and the chart title stays at 600.

**Card facts.** The values are 20 px (v0.26). The labels (Height, Footprint, Low spot) are 14 px medium (v0.27; 16 px regular in v0.26), and "Water reaches at" matches them: sentence case, 14 px medium, black (v0.27; a grey uppercase overline before).

## Engineering

- **State:** one reducer (`src/state/reducer.ts`) covers scenario, comparison, layers, view mode, selection, dialogs, panels and camera tokens. Transitions are pure and unit-tested. For example, picking a scenario equal to the comparison partner re-selects a valid partner.
- **Data loading:** the manifest is fetched and validated once, then `useCollection` lazy-loads each GeoJSON. It keeps the previous data visible while the next file loads and caches files per session. Failures are surfaced, never hidden.
- **MapView** is the only module that calls MapLibre. Long-lived event handlers read from a `live` ref, so React state changes never re-bind them.
- **Geometry:** offline in Python/GEOS (clip, repair, difference, simplify, geodesic area). In the browser, Turf handles only point-in-polygon and footprint intersection for inspection.
- **Basemap resilience:** if the style fetch fails, a plain fallback style still renders NOAA overlays, with a visible warning.
